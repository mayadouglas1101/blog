SORRY, YOU'RE JUST NOT TECHNICAL

CATEGORY: TECH + SOCIETY

I hate the cliches about female cybersecurity professionals. Cut off the first descriptor and it's a paycheck. Why not just nix the female part? Yet I made my way to yet another women in tech gathering. It's good networking. Maybe I'll even make a friend or two. They weren't the balls of anxiety you'd find at a traditional meetup. Not braced for impact. None of the bravado that comes with the DEFCON crowd. None of the credential one-upping or pop quizzing technical concepts. No gatekeeping. After small talk and formalities, they often turned the conversation over to professional development. And they'd ask. "So, are you a student?" "I'm a SOC analyst! Graduated a couple of years ago, actually." "Oh! That's great. It's been a while for me (haha) my son's about to go to college!" "Exciting! Are you nervous?" "Yeah. He's going to major in computer science at (the school she went to.)" "Cool! I'm sure he had some great pointers growing up." "I'm just a project manager though. Less of the technical side." "How's being a project manager?" "It's great. I'm the bridge between the engineers and business." huh you graduated a top five CS program 20 years ago when the dinosaurs roamed and you were forced to write assembly. no google, no claude. You go on to have a great conversation about the wonderful career she's had, and how she watched the rise of social media and the internet. Which she got to build. But she's not technical. You go onto the next person. Someone a little younger looking. Full of life. Wearing a 'she leads tech' shirt or something. "So, are you a student?" "No, I'm in the workforce. How about you?" "I do compliance for (electronics firm in the metro area.)" "That's cool! How did you get into that." "I originally did QA for the engineering team. But I bridge the gap between IT and compliance now. Not really on the technical side?" wasnt she QA next "So, are you a student?" "No, how about you?" "I do security awareness training." "How did you get into that?" "I was originally a system administrator but switched over to education. I'm a storyteller. Not the most technical." if sys admins aren't technical, lord save us all Ah. A graveyard for the technical dreams these women once had. Now they have paperwork. And some buzzwords. I went home that night and kind of gave up the whole women in tech stuff. I'd rather have some guy call me the r slur to my face than hear another line about storytelling. Everyone there was passionate enough to show up in that room. I'm not convinced that it's the paperwork that really lights them up. Now technical is ... a loaded word. Very loaded. “Technical” isn’t a description. It’s a rank. Technical vs non-technical are already treated as a binary. If you were clutching a Raspberry Pi in the womb, you’re technical. No head start? Well, you’re such a good writer! And I mean. Bridge? Storyteller? Really? Why go through the grueling coursework and jump through hoops to be handed the consolation prize of being literate. So is 99% of the country. Good writer? I hope so! Writing is like talking, but with a keyboard. And no one’s born technical. If women are less likely to adopt hobbies that require them to interact with computers (gaming, Reddit), they’d probably not know about computers as much. None of my teenage hobbies required me to buy the newest graphics card. No Linux customization is needed when you’re mostly using a Chromebook to open a couple of web apps. So I’m not that technical. Shouldn’t I just get my ‘literate’ consolation prize and become a project manager? Or maybe marketing! It’s not a demotion. It’s just different. A 100,000 dollar difference. Downwards. And by the way, the guys at your firm still won’t respect you. But they’d be so relieved that they don’t have to play along anymore! The worst thing is that this whole situation is framed as biological. I mean, not out loud. Just a hunch of mine. Using my womanly intuition powers (XX exclusive.) Because on the surface-level, being able to spot anti-forensic evidence easily is genius. Not a habit from deleting your history. And being able to read a memory dump makes you a savant. Not someone who avidly uses cheat engine. And all of this is framed as innate. Therefore no matter the credential, no matter the resume. You're either technical or you're not. In my other article, I said that I suck at IT but I'm great at security. I'll stand by that statement. Always loved my English classes and didn't take the computer ones. I'm not even standard not-that-technical. It's cranked up. So my outward facing materials have gotten applauded at every job. Technical adventures range from 'cute' to 'liability' but never innovative. Since I'll never be the guy who started hacking in middle school. But does that seal my fate? Most of the women at that meet were once me. Getting into the field isn't particularly difficult. But less than half of us are left by age 35. As a recently-wed woman in my mid-20s, I already feel the currents pulling me into a fate I don't want. I don't want to use words like 'storyteller' to tell myself a story that an administrative path was one that I chose. Being a good writer shouldn't be used against me. Being a people person shouldn't push me into sales. A career is just a career, but people reading you wrong over and over and over again gets so fucking old until one day you start believing it. And you start calling yourself 'not technical' as if the mediocre guy engineers aren't whipped up to shape by a couple of mentors and a training course. To be honest, I'd like to stay on a technical path but who's to say what the future holds. I'm just one person. But there are women like me out there who are starting to believe the narrative. I want our GRC professionals and PMs to actually like their jobs. People say I talk about free will too much, but in this life we have agency to make decisions. I just want to make the right decision, not the one I'm worn down into choosing.
