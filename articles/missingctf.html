ON THE CREATION OF 'MISSING - THE CTF'

CATEGORY: CYBERSECURITY

Honestly I think I'm at about 40-50 HTB/eJPT machines combined at this point. That's probably about average or even on the lower side for a security professional, even on the defensive side (I'm a SOC Analyst.) But I really like analog horror (like, reeeally like it) and figured that CTFs and ARGs are similar concepts in different mediums. So why not blur the lines? There were a few elements I wanted to incorporate. A missing person feels like a good hook. I spend a good chunk of time in downtown Denver. Definitely some humor. Some pop culture - The Archer is a Taylor Swift song about isolation and I felt like it was fitting in terms of Marcus and his experience. I also dropped a couple of easter eggs in for people I suspected were more likely to solve this CTF. I think cryptography-lite was a good angle to go with. Since less technical people can still put it in Claude or learn the basics of things such as Caesar cipher, base64, and even some symbolic logic. The original plan was actually to create archery bow-like sounding noises to create morse code, but I kind of figured that would be a bit obscure and cause some friction. The symbolic logic part with binary and the union symbol (that's what the coordinates were there for) were originally supposed to be something you hand solved? And there were no hints at the different symbols. But that kind of goes back to the 'what would someone do when they get to this phase.' Next time? I'd probably think a little more about narrative cohesion and tying it back into the technical aspects. Since Marcus leaving tidbits of his thoughts on FTP servers makes sense .. if you're a crazy SOC analyst. But my husband wants my next game to be about dinosaurs and I'm not what velociraptors have to do with buffer overflows. It's pretty easy to think of something, but to ship something? That's effort. And a Digital Ocean subscription. Unless you want to sacrifice a device. See ya!
