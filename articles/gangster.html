STUDIO GANGSTER

CATEGORY: TECH + SOCIETY

A studio gangster is someone who: - Portrays a tough, street-credible, or criminal persona in their public image, music, or social media — but has no genuine background in that lifestyle - "Talks the talk" but has never "walked the walk" - Performs or brags about experiences (crime, violence, street life) that are fabricated or heavily exaggerated for image purposes So like, going to DEFCON before you know what DNS is? Using Claude for technical writing? Well call me Vanilla Ice because less than three years ago, I actually went to DEFCON before I knew what DNS really did. I had a computer science degree, spare cash, a place to crash, and a dream. So I took a Frontier flight to Vegas for the first time. It's been less than three years and I now work as a security analyst 2 at a cybersecurity company. Did being a poser get me here? Am I just better at posing now? And how do I disguise myself if I ever run into the people who knew me in 2023? I've spoken to a lot of people about this phenomenon and it seems like people mistake it for imposter syndrome. It's not. It's imposter syndrome adjacent? But that's operating under the assumption that you competent to begin with. Studio gangsters are the antithesis of people with imposter syndrome. But not quite dunning kruger. Since they aren't really imposters. They never claimed to be competent in the first place. Their identity lies in affiliation rather than the craft. I think there's a certain threshold of what makes a cybersecurity studio gangster. The relationship between how committed you are to the bit, and your competence, is really what it boils down to. The bit refers to have outwardly enthusiastic or embedded you are to the culture. Sometimes it's a bit performative. Therefore, 'the bit.' Competence is self-explanatory. Here's a chart that Claude generated for me. Anything left on the line makes you a studio gangster. Anything to the right means you're safe. This is actually a relatively forgiving model. You can stay in the safe zone forever if you're simply not performative. So why not stick to apathy? It's so comfortable. Eliminate all risk. Nothing can go wrong if you never cared to begin with. The danger in sticking too close to the x-axis of this is the risk of dropping off the chart in general. The best and worst part about this field is that it's really hard. Which makes it so much more rewarding. But that takes time, effort, and dedication. And the studio gangster phenomenon is really a reflection of human behavior. Because what drives an action? A motivation. And apathy is the antithesis to motivation. So studio gangsters aren't really posers. It's just what drives people towards building a challenging skill set. Maybe the metaphor falls apart when you realize that rappers from the suburbs aren't driven to move to Compton. They're just studio gangster lifers. Maybe the real litmus test is whether the noob is moving toward the competence, or just living in the performance.
